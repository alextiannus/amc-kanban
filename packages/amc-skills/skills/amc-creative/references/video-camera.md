# Camera: motivated framing and motion

Apply during current-script rewrite under video-rewrite.md's contract. Original AMC guidance informed by video-shotcraft's purposeful shot-card approach (https://github.com/vincentwei1021/video-shotcraft); this is not an installation of its full gallery or production pipeline.

- Select framing to show evidence: establish spatial context when needed, then a detail that proves the selling point. Name the subject and start/end composition, not merely “push in” or “camera movement”.
- Use one clear motivated move when useful: a slow push toward a relevant detail, a short lateral reveal of context, or tracking that follows an existing action. State the direction, endpoint and approximate portion of the segment used. Hold still when reading text, watching a key action, or an explicit merchant request requires it.
- A crop/scale change of existing footage is an editorial reframing, not a newly photographed orbit or unseen camera angle. Without verified footage coverage, describe a proposed shot or the simpler static/crop alternative; never claim that a reference image proves an action occurred.
- Preserve the current material segment when the same subject and selling point continue through a framing change. Do not create additional upload cards for each push, detail or animation beat. Do not force a fixed number of shots.
- Cut on meaningful action, gaze, sound or information change. Avoid decorative spins, whip pans, fake parallax, speed ramps or aggressive blur that hide food/product details. Use stylized motion only when requested or clearly serving the brand and available materials.
- Describe titles and overlays as motion graphics separately from physical camera movement. Reusable shot recipes are optional vocabulary, not a mandatory effect or fixed timing for every film.
